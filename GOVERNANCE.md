# Governance

CeloHT is governed through a structure designed to balance clear accountability today with a credible path toward more decentralized community governance over time.

## Structure

```
Foundation Director → Maintainer Council → Community Contributors
```

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Foundation Director** | Strategic vision, external representation, final call in a genuine deadlock |
| **Maintainer Council** | Technical review, PR approval, day-to-day repository management |
| **Community Contributors** | Issues, pull requests, discussions — the entry point to the Maintainer Council |

The Foundation Director provides long-term vision and continuity but does not hold unilateral authority over funds, policy, or roadmap. Director-proposed changes go through the same review process as any other proposal for major or governance-level changes (see [Decision-Making](#decision-making) below).

## How the Maintainer Council Organizes Its Work

Rather than reviewing every domain with the same small group, the Council organizes its work into informal working groups by pillar and function — Education, Agent Network, Reforestation, Engineering, and Governance & Legal. These aren't a separate governance layer; they're how Council members divide domain-specific review and day-to-day decisions, reporting back to the full Council on anything with cross-cutting or foundational impact. Where other documents in this repository reference a "Working Group" for a specific pillar, that refers to this internal division of Council responsibility — see [WORKING_GROUPS.md](./WORKING_GROUPS.md).

## Decision-Making

- **Minor changes** (typo fixes, documentation clarity) — approved by any maintainer after standard review.
- **Major changes** (structural changes, policy shifts, brand changes) — require Maintainer Council consensus, discussed publicly first.
- **Governance changes** (edits to this document) — require a minimum 14-day public comment period and majority Council approval.

## Roadmap Toward Decentralization

CeloHT's governance model is designed to evolve: a lightweight elected community input process in Phase 3 of the [roadmap](./ROADMAP.md), moving toward a more formally decentralized model in Phase 4. This is a staged transition with a defined mechanism behind it, not an open-ended promise — see [ROADMAP.md](./ROADMAP.md#phase-4--maturity-2028) for the specific commitments and target dates.

## Maintainers

Individuals with merge/commit access to specific repositories, appointed based on sustained, quality contribution. Contributors who show sustained, high-quality involvement are eligible for nomination to the Maintainer Council — see [CONTRIBUTING.md](./CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Conflict Resolution

Disputes are first addressed within the relevant area of Council responsibility. Unresolved disputes escalate to the full Maintainer Council, and, where needed, the Foundation Director makes the final call in a genuine deadlock. Code-of-conduct violations follow the enforcement process in [CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md](./CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md), independent of this technical decision-making process.

## Transparency Commitments

- Governance decisions and their rationale are public
- Funding sources and allocation are reported per [PARTNERS.md](./PARTNERS.md) and [FUNDING_POLICY.md](./FUNDING_POLICY.md)
- Roadmap progress is reported through monthly community updates and [CHANGELOG.md](./CHANGELOG.md)
- No governance decision is made in a private channel without a public record of the outcome and reasoning

## Funding Governance

See [FUNDING_POLICY.md](./FUNDING_POLICY.md) for the full funding model and allocation process. No funding may be used to establish a token, staking mechanism, or investment product — see [NO_TOKEN_POLICY.md](./NO_TOKEN_POLICY.md).

## Ethics

All governance participants are expected to act in the interest of CeloHT's mission over personal, organizational, or political interest. Conflicts of interest must be disclosed, with the affected person recused from the relevant decision.
