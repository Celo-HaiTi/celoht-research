# Research Ethics

**Summary:** Ethical standards for any CeloHT research involving people, communities, or their data.
**Purpose:** Protect the communities CeloHT studies from being treated as data sources rather than participants.
**Scope:** All research involving human subjects — surveys, interviews, field observation, program data analysis.
**Audience:** Researchers, field staff, reviewers.

## Core Commitments

1. **Informed consent.** Anyone participating in a study — a survey, an interview, observed program data — understands what's being collected, why, and how it will be used, before it's collected.
2. **No coercion.** Participation in research is never a condition of accessing CeloHT's education, Agent Network, or reforestation programs. Declining to participate carries no penalty.
3. **Minimal necessary data.** Collect what the research question requires, not what might be interesting later. See [DATA_POLICY.md](./DATA_POLICY.md#data-minimization).
4. **De-identification by default.** Published research de-identifies individual participants unless a participant explicitly consents to attribution (e.g. a named success story, which follows the separate consent process in the main documentation repository's `SUCCESS_STORIES.md`).
5. **Community benefit.** Research should ultimately benefit the communities it studies, not extract value from them for external audiences alone.
6. **Honest about power dynamics.** CeloHT staff and agents hold real influence over the communities they study; research design accounts for the risk that participants may feel pressure to give favorable answers, and reports that risk as a limitation where relevant.

## Special Consideration: Vulnerable Populations

CeloHT's programs frequently involve first-time financial-tool users, some of whom may have low literacy, limited prior exposure to research processes, or economic vulnerability. Research involving these populations requires:

- Plain-language consent materials in Haitian Creole, not translated legal boilerplate
- A named contact participants can reach with questions or concerns, independent of the researcher conducting the study
- Extra caution against research designs that could expose a participant's financial situation to their community without consent

## Ethics Review

Research involving direct data collection from program participants (surveys, interviews) requires review by at least one maintainer not involved in designing the study, in addition to standard [peer review](./PEER_REVIEW.md). This is a distinct check — peer review assesses whether findings are sound; ethics review assesses whether the process of obtaining them was fair.

## What Requires Explicit Ethics Sign-Off Before Data Collection Begins

- Any survey or interview with program participants
- Any research using Agent Network transaction data at the individual (not aggregate) level
- Any research involving minors (e.g. school-based education program evaluation)

## Conflicts of Interest

Researchers disclose any financial or organizational relationship that could bias findings — including CeloHT staff studying CeloHT's own programs, which is common and not prohibited, but must be disclosed plainly in the published work, not left implicit.

## Handling Negative or Unfavorable Findings

Findings that reflect poorly on a CeloHT program are published with the same rigor as favorable findings — see [PUBLICATION_POLICY.md](./PUBLICATION_POLICY.md#publishing-unfavorable-findings). Ethics review exists partly to prevent quiet suppression of inconvenient results.

## Reporting an Ethics Concern

Email research@celoht.com. Concerns are reviewed independently of the research team involved.
