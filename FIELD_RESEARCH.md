# Field Research

**Summary:** Practical guidance for conducting research directly in CeloHT's operating communities.
**Purpose:** Field research done carelessly can damage community trust that took years to build — this document exists to prevent that.
**Scope:** Any research requiring in-person presence in a community (interviews, observation, surveys).
**Audience:** Field researchers, Agent Network coordinators supporting research.

## Before Going to the Field

- Research design and ethics review complete per [RESEARCH_ETHICS.md](./RESEARCH_ETHICS.md)
- Local Agent Network coordinator informed and, where relevant, involved in planning — they hold the community relationship, and field research that bypasses them risks damaging it
- Consent materials prepared in Haitian Creole, reviewed by a fluent speaker for clarity, not just literal translation accuracy

## Community Relationship Principles

- Research access is never assumed — it's requested, through the same trusted local channels (Agent Network members, community leaders) that CeloHT's programs already rely on
- A community that says no to a specific study is respected, fully, without pressure or repeated asks
- Research shouldn't disrupt program delivery — a cash-in/cash-out queue doesn't become a captive survey audience

## Safety

- Field researchers follow the same safety principles as Agent Network field operations — see the main documentation repository's `agent-network/emergency-procedures.md`
- No research activity proceeds if it would put a researcher or participant at physical risk
- Weather, timing, and cultural considerations (e.g. avoiding research activity during significant community events without prior coordination) are planned for, not treated as afterthoughts

## Data Collection in the Field

- Physical data collection tools (paper surveys, recorders) are secured immediately — not left accessible in transit
- Digital data collection follows the encryption and access standards in [DATA_POLICY.md](./DATA_POLICY.md#storage)
- Field notes distinguish observation from interpretation — "the agent paused before answering" is an observation; "the agent seemed uncomfortable" is an interpretation, and both are useful, but conflating them weakens the record

## Compensating Participants

Where participant time compensation is appropriate (e.g. a lengthy interview), it's modest, transparent, and paid in cUSD consistent with [NO_TOKEN_POLICY.md](https://github.com/Celo-HT/CeloHT/blob/main/NO_TOKEN_POLICY.md) — never structured as a reward tied to giving a particular answer.

## After the Field

- Debrief with the local coordinator — what worked, what strained the relationship, what to change next time
- De-identify data before it leaves secure field storage, per [DATA_POLICY.md](./DATA_POLICY.md)
- Share a plain-language summary of findings back with the community studied, where practical — research shouldn't be purely extractive
