# Methodology Notes: Financial Inclusion Research

**Summary:** Area-specific methodological notes, building on the shared framework in [../../METHODOLOGY.md](../../METHODOLOGY.md).
**Purpose:** Document choices specific to studying financial inclusion outcomes, not repeat the general framework.
**Scope:** Financial-inclusion-specific measurement and design considerations.
**Audience:** Researchers designing studies in this area.

## Defining "Inclusion" for This Research Area

Financial inclusion is measured here as a combination of **access** (can someone use CeloHT's tools if they choose to), **usage** (do they actually use them), and **outcome** (does usage change their financial position or resilience) — a program can score well on access and usage while still not demonstrating outcome, and this research area is designed to catch that gap rather than stop at usage metrics alone.

## Baseline Measurement Approach

Where feasible, studies in this area use a before/after or participant/non-participant comparison design (see [../../METHODOLOGY.md](../../METHODOLOGY.md#baseline-and-comparison)), since financial behavior varies enormously across households for reasons unrelated to CeloHT — a standalone snapshot after program exposure can't distinguish CeloHT's effect from pre-existing differences.

## Specific Confounds to Address

- **Self-selection:** people who seek out Agent Network services may differ systematically (more entrepreneurial, more mobile-savvy) from those who don't
- **Agent effects:** outcomes may vary by individual agent quality, not just program design — studies should account for agent-level variation, not treat the Agent Network as uniform
- **Seasonality:** financial behavior in Haiti has seasonal patterns (harvest cycles, remittance timing); cross-sectional snapshots should note when data was collected

## Survey Design Considerations

Financial questions can be sensitive. Survey design in this area follows [../../RESEARCH_ETHICS.md](../../RESEARCH_ETHICS.md#special-consideration-vulnerable-populations), using indirect or range-based questions where direct income/savings questions risk non-response or inaccurate answers.
