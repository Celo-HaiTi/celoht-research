# Education Research

**Summary:** Research on curriculum effectiveness and financial/digital literacy outcomes from CeloHT's education programs.
**Purpose:** Test whether CeloHT's education modules actually build the understanding they're designed to build.
**Scope:** Module completion, knowledge retention, behavior change, curriculum design effectiveness.
**Audience:** Education Working Group, researchers, partner educational institutions.

## Overview

CeloHT's founding premise is that education must come before financial access (see the main documentation repository's `EDUCATION.md`). This research area tests that premise directly: does completing CeloHT's curriculum change what people understand and how they behave, not just whether they clicked through eight modules.

## Research Questions

1. Do knowledge-check scores improve meaningfully from pre- to post-module, and does that improvement persist weeks later?
2. Does completing the wallet-safety module (Module 4) measurably change real-world scam susceptibility or safe-transaction behavior?
3. Which delivery format — in-person workshop, app-based micro-lesson — produces better retention for which audience?
4. Do train-the-trainer partners deliver the curriculum with comparable fidelity and outcomes to CeloHT's own facilitators?

## Objectives

Give the Education Working Group evidence to iterate the curriculum, rather than assuming the current 8-module design (see the main documentation repository's `education/`) is optimal by default.

## Data Sources

Module completion records, knowledge-check scores (pre/post), planned: delayed follow-up assessments, planned: behavioral proxy data (e.g. safe transaction patterns among trained vs. untrained Agent Network users).

## Methodology

Primarily survey and program-data analysis — see [../../METHODOLOGY.md](../../METHODOLOGY.md#research-types-used-at-celoht). Comparative design (trained vs. not-yet-trained cohorts) where feasible.

## Limitations

Self-selection into training (people who choose to complete a module may differ systematically from those who don't) is a persistent confound — addressed explicitly per [METHODOLOGY.md](../../METHODOLOGY.md#confounding-and-alternative-explanations) in any published finding, not ignored.

## Future Work

- Comparative study: in-person vs. app-based delivery outcomes
